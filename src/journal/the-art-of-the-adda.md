Calcutta has never been in a hurry. You see it in the way a tea stall on a side
street holds four people for two hours over cups that went cold in the first ten
minutes. You see it in the chairs dragged into a rough circle on a terrace, in a
college canteen that empties long after the last class. Nobody called the meeting.
Nobody set an agenda. It just happened, and then it kept happening.

That is adda, and it is the closest thing this city has to a civic institution.

## It is not the same as catching up

Catching up has a purpose. You meet, you exchange news, you go home. Adda has no
purpose at all, and that is the point of it. The conversation wanders from football
to politics to a cousin's wedding to whether the fish at the new place is worth the
trip. It doubles back. It contradicts itself. Someone leaves and someone else
arrives and the thread picks up without explanation.

What holds it together is not the subject. It is the willingness to stay.

## What that asks of a beer

A drink built for adda has a particular job, and it is not to be impressive.

It has to survive the second hour. A beer that announces itself on the first sip
and then sits heavily for the rest of the evening is the wrong instrument for a
conversation with no end time. What you want is something that stays interesting
while you stop paying attention to it.

That is harder to brew than it sounds. Restraint is not the absence of character,
and a beer with nothing going on gets dull faster than a loud one. The balance
sits somewhere in between: enough going on to notice when you look, quiet enough
to forget when you don't.

## Why we brew the way we do

We leave our beers unfiltered. Fine sediment settles in the bottle, and we would
rather you saw it than didn't. Filtering strips haze, and it takes flavour and
mouthfeel out along with it. Consistency is a factory value. Character is a craft
one, and we know which side of that we are on.

The same thinking shapes the range. [Blue](/blue) is a pilsner that stays crisp
through a long evening. [Witbier](/yellow) is soft and citrus-lifted, easy to sit
with. [Strong](/strong) is the one for when the conversation has gone somewhere and
nobody is leaving yet. Different evenings, different pours.

## The room matters more than the drink

Any honest brewer will tell you this. The beer is a small part of what makes a
good evening — the company does most of the work, and the room does the rest. A
table you can actually hear across. Chairs nobody is waiting for. Staff who do not
hover with the bill.

We find Caldera in places like that more often than not, which is probably not a
coincidence. You can see where we are pouring on the [Where to Find](/find) page.

## Slow down and stay a while

Calcutta's genius has always been for the unhurried. The afternoon that becomes an
evening. The chair nobody gets up from. The argument you have had before and will
have again, and enjoy just as much the fourth time.

Pour something you don't have to concentrate on. Then stop concentrating on it.
